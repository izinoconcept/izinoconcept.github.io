Petroleum-Sector Registration in Nigeria: A Practical Document Checklist for Service Companies

By the i.zino concept team · Published 28 October 2026 · 8 min read

Companies that want to supply goods or services to the oil and gas industry in Nigeria quickly find that registration is not one task but a chain of them, where each document depends on another. Getting the order right saves weeks. Below is the sequence we use as a working framework, based on supporting clients through industry registration, including NSITF employer registration and clearance documentation.

Check for changes. Regulators, forms, fees and portals change. Treat this as a map of the process, and confirm current requirements directly with each agency or with a qualified adviser before you submit.

First, a correction many older guides still get wrong

If you are reading a guide that tells you to apply to the Department of Petroleum Resources (DPR), it is out of date. The government scrapped the DPR in 2021 and created two new regulators: the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission (NUPRC) for upstream operations, and the Nigerian Midstream and Downstream Petroleum Regulatory Authority (NMDPRA) for midstream and downstream. The right regulator for your permit depends on the kind of work you will do, so confirm which one applies to your service category. Old certificates may still carry DPR branding, and clients sometimes ask for them, but new applications go to the successor bodies.

Similarly, the Federal Inland Revenue Service (FIRS) became the Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) on 1 January 2026 under the new tax laws. Names on forms and portals are changing, and some guides still use the old ones.

Stage 1: Your corporate foundation (CAC)

Everything else rests on a properly incorporated company. Have these ready and consistent:

  • Certificate of incorporation from the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC)
  • Memorandum and Articles of Association
  • Particulars of directors and shareholders, including any information the CAC requires on persons with significant control
  • A current CAC status report (many pre-qualification exercises ask for a recent one)
  • Evidence of your registered office address

Make sure the company name, address and director names are written identically on every document. Small differences are a common reason for rejected submissions.

Stage 2: Tax registration and compliance

  • Tax Identification Number (TIN)
  • Evidence of tax compliance, such as a tax clearance certificate or its current equivalent, issued by the relevant federal or state revenue authority
  • Filed returns and, where required, audited financial statements

With the changes to the national tax framework, confirm the current name of the issuing authority and the current form of the compliance evidence before you apply.

Stage 3: Employer registrations

NSITF (Employees' Compensation Scheme). Under the Employees' Compensation Act 2010, employers contribute 1% of total monthly payroll to the Employees' Compensation Fund, and the contribution comes from the employer, not from staff deductions. Registration uses NSITF's employer forms, and the compliance certificate is issued on submission of the request letter, your CAC certificate, the completed forms and evidence of payment. NSITF indicates a typical processing time of about five working days once everything is complete and verified. Because the certificate depends on your CAC documents and on payroll records, start this early.

Other employer obligations. Depending on your size and structure, tenders often also ask for evidence of pension compliance and of Industrial Training Fund (ITF) contributions. Check whether and how they apply to you.

Stage 4: The industry permit from the regulator

Once the foundation is in place, you apply to the relevant regulator for the permit that matches your service category. Categories differ in the technical capacity expected. Typical supporting material includes:

  • A company profile and organisation chart
  • CVs and certifications of key technical staff
  • A list of equipment, tools or facilities relevant to the service
  • Your health, safety and environment (HSE) policy
  • Evidence of premises
  • A bank reference and recent financial statements
  • Your NSITF, tax and CAC documents from the earlier stages

Requirements vary by category, so ask the regulator for the current checklist for the specific service you plan to offer.

Stage 5: Nigerian content registration

Operators and major contractors usually expect service companies to be registered with the Nigerian Content Development and Monitoring Board (NCDMB), commonly through the Nigerian Oil and Gas Industry Content Joint Qualification System. Registration there is often a pre-qualification requirement for tenders, and it also asks for evidence of ownership, local content and capacity. Confirm the current process with the Board.

Stage 6: Build a compliance file and a renewal calendar

Many certificates and permits have to be renewed periodically. Keep one organised file, paper and digital, with every certificate, its issue date and its expiry, and set reminders well ahead. An expired certificate discovered during a tender is far more expensive than a diary entry.

The mistakes that cause repeat submissions

  • Following an old checklist. Using DPR-era instructions or former agency names.
  • Inconsistent details. Names, addresses or directors that differ across documents.
  • Wrong order. Applying for an item before its prerequisite exists, for example NSITF clearance before the CAC documents are complete.
  • Incomplete payroll or financial records. These are needed for NSITF and for pre-qualification.
  • Missing expiry dates. Submitting a certificate that lapses before the process finishes.
