What a Medical Retainership Agreement Usually Covers

A medical retainership agreement sets out how a company (often in oil & gas or a large employer) will use a designated hospital or clinic for staff medical needs. Typical elements include the scope of services, response times, billing arrangements, confidentiality and how emergencies are handled. Clear drafting at the start reduces disputes later and helps both the provider and the client plan capacity.

When we facilitate these agreements we encourage both sides to document service levels, approved facilities and communication contacts so that day-to-day operations remain smooth.

Customer Service · Practical Tips

Turning Enquiries into Long-Term Clients

Many organisations lose potential clients at the first point of contact—slow replies, unclear answers or a lack of follow-up. A simple, consistent process for handling enquiries and complaints can change that. We recommend defining ownership of each channel, setting realistic response times, and creating a short internal checklist so every staff member knows what “good” looks like.

The goal is not perfection on day one; it is reliable, professional handling that makes clients feel heard and valued.

Business Setup · Nigeria

Preparing for Industry Registration in the Petroleum Sector

Companies that wish to offer services to the oil industry in Nigeria often need formal registration with the relevant regulatory bodies. Documentation requirements can include company records, tax clearance, NSITF registration and other clearances. Starting the process with a complete checklist and accurate paperwork saves time and reduces the risk of repeated submissions.

We have supported clients through these steps and can advise on the sequence that typically works best.

Partnerships

Why Hospitals Benefit from Corporate Retainership

For private hospitals and clinics, a retainership with a stable corporate client brings predictable patient volume and a clearer cash-flow picture. It also creates an opportunity to standardise service pathways for that client’s staff. The key is matching capacity and specialisations to the client’s actual needs so the arrangement remains sustainable for both parties.
